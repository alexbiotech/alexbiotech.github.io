How I get agents to finish the work

My expectation of an agent is straightforward: carry the task through to something I can use and inspect. Even a thoughtful response can leave sources to find, an artifact to build, and errors to fix. Someone has to keep that work moving.

I start by defining the question, goal, constraints, and acceptance criteria. For a personal website, those criteria include what a visitor can find, whether the pages work, and whether the writing matches my actual experience. Discussing the goal with an agent can help, but the discussion needs to lead to a concrete deliverable.

I then separate execution from review. The agents doing the work gather material, build a version, and respond to feedback. Reviewers use the same criteria to inspect the finished artifact and its original sources. They identify specific problems, explain their consequences, and suggest revisions. Boris Cherny’s discussion of verification loops is useful here: an agent needs a way to observe whether its work is succeeding as it continues. Original

Long tasks also need a handoff. A useful handoff lets the next person or agent recover the goal, important decisions, completed work, open questions, and file locations. I once wrote on X that people could learn from context compression, too. Returning after a day away is easier when I can recover the state of the task before taking the next step. Anthropic’s account of structured notes and context management offers techniques to borrow. Original

I also like to have different models examine the same question, especially when one is asked to find counterexamples or omissions. A disagreement tells me where to investigate. Resolving a factual question still takes sources, data, or observed behavior. Reviewers need access to that material as well, so they can examine the work directly.

Agents can handle retrieval, organization, and repetitive work while I focus on the goal, consequential decisions, and feedback. The value of adding another agent depends on whether it helps resolve the problem.

With writing, I still want to think through the claims and the language myself. Tools can find errors, remove repetition, and help me see why a sentence is weak. I decide what stays. Before calling the task finished, I need to look at the actual result, too.
