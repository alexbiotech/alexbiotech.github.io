AI4Bio: What evidence comes after a prediction?

I once asked on X whether scaling laws still work in biology, and wanted examples of AI reaching industry. Now I want to separate the questions more carefully. When data, compute, or model size increases, which task improves? Where does that improvement come from, and under what conditions does it hold?

For biological questions, the conditions are part of what I want to understand. Can a model make a useful prediction when the same molecule is considered in a different cell state or environment? If performance falls, is the missing piece relevant data, a description of the conditions, or an understanding of the problem? I want evidence that helps distinguish those explanations.

Which decision would the prediction change?

I also want to know whether AI can help researchers reach a worthwhile decision sooner. Target discovery, data analysis, new indications for existing drugs, and choosing the next study may offer different kinds of value. Calling all of them faster research can obscure which part has actually become faster.

If a model proposes a target, I want to know how researchers then judge whether it deserves study. If analysis becomes faster, I want to know whether researchers understand the important differences in their data sooner. For a proposed indication or next experiment, I want to know whether it changed the original choice and whether later evidence supported that change.

These are questions I want to keep comparing, using the evidence appropriate to each task. A more accurate prediction may narrow the possibilities. Whether it saves research time or produces a more useful result also depends on what happens after it enters a decision.

Different cases answer different questions

Arc’s overview of Evo 2 includes evaluations of human variants. I want to know what transferable patterns a model learns from its data and how useful its predictions remain in a new setting. Overview

OpenAI, PerturbAI, and Scripps Research used Rosalind to reanalyze existing mouse brain cell data and propose leads for further testing. Isomorphic Labs has reported preclinical activity for a molecule selected by IsoDDE. One case asks whether analysis can reveal a useful question; the other asks whether a computational choice receives experimental support. Neither answers a question about clinical efficacy. Rosalind · IsoDDE

The Virtual Biotech examines targets and outcomes using historical clinical-trial information and revisits terminated trials. That raises another question for me: can records of failure help revise a hypothesis or suggest a new indication worth testing? Paper

How far has the evidence progressed?

For an industry example, I want to distinguish where AI contributed from what happened to the candidate afterward. A 2025 paper on rentosertib reported a randomized, placebo-controlled phase 2a trial involving 71 participants over 12 weeks. That provides evidence from human research on the candidate. Larger and longer trials are still needed to assess long-term safety and efficacy. Paper

The case makes me want to ask what the clinical evidence and the evidence about discovery each answer. After a study reports how the candidate performed, what comparisons would help establish AI’s contribution to discovery efficiency? The questions a case raises can deserve more attention than a broad label of success.

In future research, I want clearer connections among predictions, hypotheses, and results. What explanation does a prediction suggest? What evidence supports it? Why does it remain useful, or begin to fail, under new conditions? My interest in scaling comes back to whether it helps us understand more and choose the next step on better grounds.
