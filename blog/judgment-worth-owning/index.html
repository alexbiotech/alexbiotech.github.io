Forming a judgment I can stand behind

Claude, GPT, Grok, Gemini. Sometimes I want to put the same question to each of them. I want different perspectives, and I worry that a model’s training or search may leave something important out. With an unfamiliar subject, it can be difficult to tell how much an answer covers.

Then there are more answers to assess. Which repeat the same point in different words? Which add evidence? Which disagreements affect the conclusion? If I simply read each answer and look for another, the original question may remain unresolved.

I want to examine the reasons. When several models agree, are they relying on the same material? When they disagree, do they have different facts, interpret the evidence differently, or answer different questions? Those distinctions deserve more attention than the number of agreeing responses. A factual claim takes me back to its source. A choice also requires me to consider my own goals.

Write down what I think first

One practice I want to develop is writing down my current answer and its uncertainties before reading other answers. Afterward, I can ask which information changed my view. If my view did not change, did the reasons become stronger, or did I simply like an answer that agreed with me?

Independent judgment requires listening to others. An objection may identify exactly what I most need to examine. Accepting it still takes understanding on my part. I want to be able to explain why I am willing to act on a judgment and what would make me revise it.

This applies to personal questions as well. What is worth pursuing? How do I understand the world? How do I want to live? I want to read widely and develop judgment and taste of my own. Other people’s experience can give me ideas. My choices still need to fit my circumstances.

When a new trend draws my attention, I want to ask another question. Where does it meet my interests and strengths? Is there a question within it that deserves sustained thought? Forming a judgment I can begin acting on, then revising it through experience and feedback, makes better use of finite time than waiting to read every view.
