Making up my own mind

Claude, GPT, Grok, Gemini—sometimes I’ll throw the exact same question at all four. I want to hear different angles, and I’m always a little wary that any single model’s training or search left something important out. When you’re walking into unfamiliar territory, it’s hard to tell how much of the picture one answer is actually showing you.

Of course, then you’re stuck sorting through four answers. Which ones are just saying the same thing in different words? Which one actually brought new evidence to the table? Which disagreements matter for the final call? If I just skim one answer and immediately open the next tab, the original question never actually gets settled.

What I’m really after are the reasons underneath. When three models agree, are they just echoing the same source? When they disagree, do they have different facts, are they reading the same evidence differently, or are they quietly answering slightly different questions? That tells me a lot more than a headcount. For facts, I go back to the source. For decisions, I have to bring my own goals back into the room.

Write down what I think first

One habit I’m trying to build is jotting down my own rough take—and where I’m unsure—before reading anyone else’s answer. That way, when I’m done reading, I can tell what actually moved the needle. And if my view didn’t budge, I can ask myself honestly: did the evidence get stronger, or did I just enjoy hearing someone agree with me?

Thinking for yourself doesn’t mean tuning everyone out. A sharp objection often points straight at the exact thing I need to check. Still, accepting it has to run through my own head first. By the end, I want to be able to explain why I’m willing to act on a call—and what would convince me I was wrong.

The same thing holds for bigger personal questions: what’s worth chasing, how the world works, how I want to live. I want to read widely and build up my own judgment and taste. Other people’s stories can spark ideas, sure, but my choices still have to fit the life I actually have.

And whenever a new trend catches my eye, I try to push one step further: where does this cross paths with what I care about and what I’m good at? Is there a real problem here worth sticking with? Making a good-enough call to start acting—and then updating it from real feedback—beats sitting on the sidelines waiting to read every opinion on earth.
