Leaving context for my future self

Sleep on a problem, and by morning I might still remember the conclusion but have lost the trail that got me there: which other options I weighed, why I ruled out the other approach, what I was about to try next. I’ve wondered whether a daily journal could work as a handoff to tomorrow’s me. I haven’t cracked the format yet.

Whenever an idea hits, I jot it down on my phone. Thoughts slip away fast, so capturing them saves me from trusting memory alone. Still, a one-line conclusion is rarely enough to pick a project back up a few days later. I need at least a bit of the reasoning that sat behind it.

What helps me pick back up?

What I want to try is leaving a short wrap-up note whenever I finish a stretch of thinking: the question I was working on, my main call so far, what backs it up, and what’s still fuzzy. And while the next move is still sharp in my head, write down where to start tomorrow.

It doesn’t need to be a blow-by-blow transcript. If I already ruled out a dead end, noting why keeps me from walking straight back into it next week. And if the evidence is still shaky, that uncertainty belongs in the note too. Otherwise I’ll come back later and remember a rough hunch as settled fact.

Maybe that lives in a journal, maybe in plain notes. I’m still figuring out the sweet spot: enough detail to jump right back in, without turning note-taking into a second job. Whether I can resume the work matters far more than whether the archive looks neat.

Running long tasks with AI agents made this click for me. An agent has to curate its context if it’s going to pick up a complex job in a fresh session. Humans aren’t that different: we get interrupted, we sleep, we forget. Looking at my own workflow that way makes me much more deliberate about what I leave on the desk for next time.

Good notes also give me a way to zoom out on my own thinking. Why did that direction look so promising back then? What finally made me change my mind? A bare conclusion erases the whole journey. I want to do the work in the first person, and then step back later as an outside observer to spot what the person in the trenches missed.
