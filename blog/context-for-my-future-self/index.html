Leave context for tomorrow’s self

How do I keep a long project moving? AI context management has made me reconsider the role of notes and reflection.

The more I use AI, the more I want to examine my own thinking through some of the methods that help agents work well. Clear goals, manageable tasks, feedback, and revision are useful human abilities too. Thinking of each person as their own agent gives me a useful perspective on action and learning.

Continuity is one challenge. After spending a long time on a question, I can return after a night’s sleep with the conclusion but lose the thread of the reasoning. Why did this direction seem promising? Which alternatives had already been considered? What was supposed to happen next? A long project needs the ability to resume thought as much as the ability to begin it.

Save enough to continue

I want to try leaving a short handoff for my future self. It only needs to answer a few questions:

  • What am I trying to resolve, and why is it worth doing?
  • What is my most important current judgment, and what supports it?
  • What remains unclear, and where should I begin next?

A journal, a note, or a quick recording could hold that information. The format is something to try out. What matters is leaving a thread while the thinking is still clear, so there is something to pick up later.

Make changes in judgment visible

Saving the reasons also makes it easier to examine what I learned. Which assumptions held up? Which did the results overturn? Which goals need to change? A record of completed actions can leave out the changes in judgment that made those actions useful.

I also want to preserve an observer’s perspective. While working, I am concerned with moving forward. When I look back, I can examine whether the direction still makes sense. Taking turns between those perspectives can connect action and reflection.
