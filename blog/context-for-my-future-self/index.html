Leaving context for my future self

After a night’s sleep, I may remember a conclusion but lose the reasoning that led to it. Which possibilities had I considered? Why had I rejected another approach? What was I about to do next? I have wondered whether a journal could serve as a handoff to tomorrow’s self. I have not found the right approach yet.

When an idea occurs, I record it on my phone. A thought can arrive quickly, and saving it means I do not have to rely entirely on memory. Continuing a piece of work later often requires more than a sentence stating the conclusion, though. I also need some of the reasons behind it.

What would let me continue?

I want to try leaving a short record at the end of a stretch of thought. It could hold the current question, my most important judgment, what supports it, and what remains unresolved. The next place to begin is also worth writing down while it is clear.

The record need not reproduce every step. If I have rejected an approach, saving the reason may keep me from working through it again. If the evidence is incomplete, that uncertainty belongs in the record as well. Otherwise, I may later remember a conclusion as more certain than it was.

A journal is one format I have considered. Notes could work, too. I still want to find out how much detail is enough to resume the work without making the record too much work to maintain. Whether I can continue matters more than whether the format looks complete.

Long AI tasks have made this question clearer to me. An agent needs to organize its context to continue working with limited information. People also experience interruptions and forgetting. The comparison makes me pay more attention to whether I have left useful information for the next beginning.

A record also gives me a way to step back from my own judgment. Why did I believe a direction was promising then? What later changed my mind? The final conclusion leaves out that process. I want to do the work from a first-person perspective, then look back as an observer and ask what the person doing it may have missed.
