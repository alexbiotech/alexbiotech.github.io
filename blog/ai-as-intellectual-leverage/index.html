AI as intellectual leverage

AI gives more people a practical path from an idea to something useful. The better we understand a problem, the more we can ask of the tools.

A shorter distance to making

I see AI as intellectual leverage. It extends what one person can understand and make. An idea often crosses several kinds of work before it becomes useful. It may need analysis, code, a clear explanation, and a usable interface. Models and agents help connect these tasks and make a first attempt more accessible.

An ordinary example

Imagine someone who wants to understand a household's electricity use. They have monthly bills and a question, but little programming experience. With AI, they can explore the data, write a small analysis, and build a chart. The useful result is a view that helps them decide what to investigate or change. That purpose gives the technical work its direction.

Judgment travels with the tools

The person still needs to ask whether the records cover the same dates, whether prices have changed, and whether a seasonal pattern explains the apparent increase. A polished chart can leave these questions unanswered. Knowing the subject helps them notice what to check; a small working version gives them something concrete to inspect. Learning and making can then reinforce each other.

What becomes possible

Lower barriers give more people a chance to try a useful idea before committing to months of specialized training. The payoff may be a modest tool that fits one person's need. I care about that expansion of possibility. Human judgment sets the purpose, chooses what deserves attention, and evaluates the result. AI helps carry that judgment further into the world.
