AI

I see AI as intellectual leverage. Models and agents can extend what one person is able to do.

Good judgment makes the tools more useful

Knowing the subject, asking better questions, and judging the result all matter. I want AI to amplify that understanding and help turn it into effective action.

More people can build

AI gives more people a practical way to analyze data, write code, and create designs. I see it as a way for one person to work across skills that once felt out of reach.

What one person can make

Data analysis

Clean a table, explore a pattern, and turn a question into a reproducible analysis.

Software and tools

Build a small tool around a real need, then improve it through actual use.

Design and explanation

Try interfaces and diagrams that make an idea easier to understand and use.

Scientific exploration

Compare ideas, surface assumptions, and identify a useful next question.

From an idea to something that works

I am interested in the whole loop: clarify an idea, choose models and tools, build a first version, test it, and improve it. As AI gets better at programming, more of the digital things I can imagine become things I can make.

  1. 01

    Define the goal

    Describe who needs the result and what it should help them do.

  2. 02

    Choose the tools

    Pick models, agents, and methods around the problem.

  3. 03

    Make a first version

    Create something small enough to test and concrete enough to judge.

  4. 04

    Test and adapt

    Use the result to revise the plan and decide what deserves more effort.

Information, feedback, and agency

Alongside using new models and agents, I return to Wiener, Shannon, Turing, and von Neumann. Their work helps me think about how information, computation, and feedback become purposeful behavior.

A question I bring to research

How can AI help propose, compare, and explain scientific designs? I care about connecting a model’s suggestion to a clear hypothesis and a useful next measurement.
