Biotechnology

IdeasResearch directionsSelected work

I am interested in how biological systems can read information and carry out a function in the right cells and conditions. Synthetic biology, RNA, and programmable cells are the directions through which I explore that question.

RNA carries information and participates in regulation. A sequence’s behavior in a cell depends on translation, stability, cell state, and delivery. I want to connect computational design with measurements along that path, so each round of design can learn from real feedback.

Questions I am pursuing

Can cell state become an input to a biological program?

Which signals distinguish the states that matter? When those signals are connected to RNA switches or regulatory elements, can the system respond under the intended conditions with a well-defined range of function?

How do we get from sequence to function?

Which measurement corresponds to a prediction of structure, expression, or stability? Which design rules still hold when the cell state, molecular context, or delivery conditions change?

Where does scaling work in AI4Bio?

Can more data, compute, or model capacity keep improving a defined biological task? I am especially interested in whether those improvements carry over to new conditions and to the measured performance of prospective designs.

How far has an industry example progressed?

I want to follow the public evidence: what decision AI contributed to, which stage a candidate reached, what was measured, and what evidence is still needed. Original papers, trial records, and comparable results tell me more than the label “AI-driven.”

Bringing models into an experimental loop

I want to start with a function that can be measured reliably, use a model to propose candidates, and compare candidates in prospective experiments to see whether the model helps select better designs. Baselines, failures, and the conditions of each result belong in the next round.
