I want therapeutic systems that sense the state of the body or its cells and act where and when needed. I also want to control how long they work and when they stop.

RNA, DNA, or proteins are possible materials for these systems. My doctoral research focused on programmable translation control and delivery vector design for circular RNA.

Biology in context: regulation, editing, and design

Cell state and control

Which cell states should a system respond to?

Can the information a system detects distinguish the cell states that matter? Do those distinctions still hold in different cells or environments?

A pulse or a sustained response?

Some uses call for a brief output; others need an effect that lasts. I want to understand how response strength and duration relate to cell state, beyond whether a signal appears at the start.

When should the effect end?

When is the function no longer needed? Can the system clear naturally, or can a person stop its activity? I also want to understand what effects remain afterward.

Immune regulation

For postdoctoral research, I hope to focus more closely on immune regulation. I am interested in clinical research on vaccines, cancer vaccines, and CAR-T therapies: when an immune response helps, how long it should persist, and when it needs to stop.

AI4Bio

Can more data, compute, or model capacity improve predictions across cell states and conditions? If so, why, and do the resulting hypotheses withstand testing? These are questions I want to pursue.

I also want to compare where AI helps in target discovery, data analysis, finding new indications for existing drugs, and using results to guide the next study. Each task needs evidence about which part may become faster and whether the model helps researchers make better decisions.
